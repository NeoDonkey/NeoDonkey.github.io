The books verify themselves.

A company here is a folder under version control. Every posting is a signed commit that names the written rule which authorized it, so an auditor does not have to take our word for anything. Neither do you.

Checked by tools we did not write

git fsck --strict and ssh-keygen -Y verify read the folder and confirm the chain. They have no stake in the claim and no knowledge of this project.

History is the system

Check out the first commit and you get a readable company, not a log file.

Nachvollziehbarkeit as a property, not a promise

German bookkeeping law asks whether a posting can be traced to the rule that permitted it. Here that is enforced when the entry is written: an operation no written rule governs is refused, and the rule that allowed it is named in the commit. The demo prints the check on every run.

Who this is for.

A good fit

  • You have been quoted a seven figure migration and want to see the alternative
  • You can read a commit, or you employ someone who can
  • Your auditor asks questions you would rather answer with evidence
  • You want the option of leaving without asking anyone's permission

Not a fit yet

  • You need DATEV or XRechnung export in production this quarter
  • You want a vendor to call when something breaks at midnight
  • You need published benchmarks before you will evaluate anything
  • You are looking for a hosted product with an account and a password
