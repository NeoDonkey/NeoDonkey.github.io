An approval that outlived its subject

A change was approved. Then it changed. Then it shipped on the old approval. Every auditor reading this already knows the ending.

On the morning of 19 August a change went into NeoDonkey carrying a review that had rejected it. Not a review that missed something. A review that said, in writing, that the change was not fit to merge. The system then announced, also in writing, that nothing merges without a clean review.

Here is the whole event, from the log: 

10:30:13  review round 1 : CLEAN            approval granted
          a new commit is pushed
10:35:00  build green on the new commit
10:35:03  MERGED
10:35:55  review round 2 : CHANGES_REQUIRED
10:36:03  "Nothing merges until a review comes back clean"

Fifty five seconds. The approval was granted at 10:30 for a version of the work that stopped existing before 10:35. Nobody withdrew it, because nothing in the system understood that it should be withdrawn. The approval was attached to the request. The reviewer had been looking at the contents.

Why a finance person should care about a build system

Strip the engineering out and this is a purchase requisition approved at one amount, edited afterwards, and paid at the new one. It is a payment run signed off on Monday and released on Thursday against a changed beneficiary list. It is a credit limit approved for one exposure and then quietly reused for another.

Controls people have a name for the whole family: the approval and the thing approved drifted apart, and nothing in the system noticed. It is one of the most common material weaknesses there is, and it almost never looks like fraud from the inside. It looks like a process that worked, because every individual step did work.

The reviewer was right. The build was green. The merge rule was correct. The approval simply outlived the thing it was granted for.

What made it visible

The second review, the one that arrived fifty five seconds too late, found that three of the documents in that change duplicated three that already existed. That rule had been added the previous day and it worked exactly as designed. It just had no way to reach back through a decision that had already been executed.

What made the whole thing legible was not cleverness. It was that the sequence above is recorded. Every state the change passed through, who or what moved it, and at which second, is in the repository. Reconstructing the failure took one pass through a log that nobody had to be asked for.

That is the property this product is built around. In most business systems the equivalent question, which approval authorised this posting and was it still valid at the moment the posting was made, is answerable in principle and expensive in practice. It ends in a ticket to a vendor.

The fix, and the honest part

Two changes. Any modification to a piece of work now withdraws the standing approval, and only a fresh clean review of that exact version restores it. A second process independently strips approval from anything carrying an open objection, because the withdrawal and the build run at the same time and a very fast build could still win the race.

What that does not fix: approvals in NeoDonkey are still checked at merge, not continuously. If a system can hold a stale approval for fifty five seconds it can hold one for fifty five milliseconds, and the second version of this bug will be harder to see than the first. That is why the change is written up rather than quietly patched.

The scorecard for version 1.0 currently reads five of ten conditions met, one partial, four unmet. It is generated from the test suite rather than written by hand, which is the only reason it is worth reading.
